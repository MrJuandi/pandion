# Data, provenance, physical calibration, and observation validity

**Status:** design checklist. Candidate sources below have different scales and licenses; inclusion requires a source-specific review.

## 1. Dataset contract

Each input layer and derived product must record: provider, product/version, acquisition or validity time, download time, spatial and temporal resolution, CRS, geographic coverage, units, processing steps, missing-data mask, uncertainty representation, license/redistribution terms, checksum, and whether it is observed, inferred, or synthetic. Scenario manifests must preserve these fields and the exact train/validation/test assignment.

No data layer is promoted from “inferred” to “observed” because it is displayed on a real map. Fuel models derived from land cover require a documented crosswalk and uncertainty; land cover is not itself a measured fuel load.

## 2. Candidate sources for Spanish scenarios

| Need | Candidate | Appropriate use and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Elevation | [IGN/PNOA terrain products](https://pnoa.ign.es/pnoa-lidar/productos-a-descarga) | Static terrain input at declared resolution. Verify coverage, vertical datum, and resampling. |
| Land cover / forest type | [Copernicus Forest Type](https://land.copernicus.eu/en/products/high-resolution-layer-forests-and-tree-cover/forest-type-2021-raster-10-m-100-m-europe-3-yearly) and [CLC+ Backbone](https://library.land.copernicus.eu/products/CLCplus_Backbone_2021_PUM_v1.html) | Spatial prior for fuel classification, with product date and uncertainty. Not a direct fire-behavior fuel measurement. |
| Background imagery | [Copernicus Sentinel-2](https://documentation.dataspace.copernicus.eu/Data/SentinelMissions/Sentinel2.html) | Multispectral context or derived vegetation products. Revisits occur over days, so imagery cannot be treated as a live five-minute fire map. Clouds and smoke can obscure observation. |
| Active-fire detection | [NASA FIRMS](https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/) | Delayed, sparse hotspots. Global near-real-time detections may be available within three hours of acquisition; they are not a continuously observed perimeter. |
| Weather observations | [AEMET OpenData](https://www.aemet.es/en/datos_abiertos/AEMET_OpenData) | Historical or current station/forecast products subject to availability, interpolation and valid timestamps. |
| Historical meteorological context | [ERA5 hourly data](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/forecasts/datasets/era5-hourly-data-single-levels-1940-present) | Reanalysis useful for historical experiments; its grid is much coarser than tactical fire cells and it is not an operational minute-by-minute measurement. |

Protected assets, roads, water access, airspace, aircraft performance and operational constraints need their own sourced datasets or clearly marked initial approximations. Licensing and redistribution must be reviewed before including files in a public repository.

## 3. Physical model validation

The sourced Rothermel equation and CL-415 reference figures selected for initial study are recorded in [PHYSICAL_MODEL_V0.md](PHYSICAL_MODEL_V0.md). Published component values do not validate the assembled simulator.

The executable `examples/synthetic_aircraft.json` has `provenance: "synthetic"` and local metre coordinates. Its ground speeds and refill duration are test inputs without empirical provenance. The v0.1 replay records a seed but uses no randomness. Passing its deterministic checks establishes accounting and time behavior only, not aircraft or fire realism.

`examples/synthetic_fire.json` likewise declares synthetic provenance and local coordinates. Its per-cell base spread rates and wind/slope coefficients are unsourced test values; no fuel model, moisture measurement, or observational fire perimeter is present. The replay stores a SHA-256 of the input scenario JSON for traceability. Directional-response tests are structural checks, not physical calibration. Actual spread-model validation, sensitivity to grid resolution, and held-out incident comparison remain open.

The FM1 example uses a **published stylized fuel model**, but its assignment to every cell, constant 6% dead moisture, flat terrain, 2 m/s midflame wind, ignition, and horizon are synthetic. FM1 load, depth, SAV, extinction moisture, and heat content come from Anderson (1982) and Andrews (2018), as recorded in [PHYSICAL_MODEL_V0.md](PHYSICAL_MODEL_V0.md). The implementation reproduces the approximate 4 mi/h reference in Andrews figure 16. A fixed-domain 100–5 m grid comparison is recorded there; it is a numerical sensitivity check, not calibration to an observed incident. Geographic fuel mapping, observed-weather conversion to midflame wind, independent perimeter validation, and convergence under heterogeneous conditions remain open.

The `standard-fuel-0.1` example adds a direct grid of Scott and Burgan numeric fuel codes and a Pyretechnics calculation. This is an input contract, not a geographic fuel map: GR1 and SH1 are hand-assigned on a local synthetic grid. A real scenario requires an explicit, documented vegetation-to-fuel crosswalk with uncertainty, independently sourced six-class moisture, midflame-wind conversion, terrain alignment, and validation against held-out perimeters. A geographic land-cover crosswalk is implemented in `nightjar.cems_catalog`; independently matched moisture/wind and physical validation remain open. The [standard-fuel path](STANDARD_FUEL_PATH.md) records the current contract and evidence sequence.

An audited candidate observed series is documented in [VALIDATION_EMSR578.md](VALIDATION_EMSR578.md): three Copernicus EMSR578 AOI01 cumulative burned-area polygons with source ZIP checksums and image acquisition times. Under Decision D-34, this is generalized into a multi-incident catalog (`nightjar.cems_catalog`) incorporating EMSR578 (Artesa de Segre), EMSR590 (Monfragüe), EMSR592 (Sierra de Mijas), and EMSR600 (Vall d'Ebo). The universal geodesy module (`nightjar.geodesy`) projects across UTM Zones 29N, 30N, and 31N using exact Krüger series without GIS dependencies. The geographic family builder requires all four source bases by default; `--raw-dir data/raw/cems` expects the four `<activation>_del.zip` files and performs no automatic download. Reserved scenarios alternate EMSR592 and EMSR600, with at least two reserved scenarios required. Explicit `--synthetic` generation marks both manifest and scenarios `synthetic_fixture`; it supports offline checks, not incident evidence. Output into a non-empty directory is rejected to prevent stale or mixed family membership. Each real base records the computed source ZIP SHA-256 (checked against the catalog before rasterization), product stage/code/filename, official URL and activation page, image acquisition time, CRS, units, and raster resolution. Derived scenarios retain the base hash, canonical parent hash, crop indices/origin/window, and their own canonical hash in the family manifest. Fuels remain an inferred land-cover crosswalk; wind, moisture, and survey timing are synthetic; topography is explicitly flat (no DEM is currently required). The satellite acquisition timestamp applies to the product's mapped static perimeter; it does not reconstruct a continuously observed active-front chronology or represent a live controller observation. Geographic PPO requires a complete, valid, manifest-matched split of the requested source kind; fixture loading is explicit and requires a previously generated synthetic family. Training/selection reports identify scenario IDs, hashes, source kinds, and directory. These ingestion checks do not validate fuels, terrain, weather, suppression, or physical performance.

**Spatial sampling contract:** raster origins are southwest cell boundaries in the projected CRS; rows increase north and columns east. CEMS source layers are tested at cell centers (`origin + (index + 0.5) * resolution`). Tactical crops retain exact source-array slices and advance the boundary origin by crop indices times resolution, with edge clamping for windows larger than the map. Discrete DROP actions use local `[column * resolution, row * resolution]` cell-anchor proxies; the executor indexes the selected cell and replay UTM coordinates add the crop origin. These are proxy positions, not measured physical drop centers, and no existing action or aircraft trajectory was shifted. Source-cell centers and proxy anchors intentionally remain distinct metadata concepts.

The generated fire grid retains the same cropped `water_mask` as an explicit static layer. Fuel code 0 is not a nodata mask: it conflates water, other nonburnable land cover, unmapped classes, and unavailable source coverage. Determining per-cell data coverage requires an independent source-coverage definition; it cannot be inferred from fuel code 0 alone. Flat elevation is an explicit landscape assumption; no DEM is required by the current builder.

**Geographic data verification:** independent end-to-end verification generated 24 static maps from the four locally cached CEMS ZIPs and matched their registered SHA-256 digests. Persisted scenario content hashes, embedded hashes, and manifest hashes agreed for all 24. Direct source reconstruction also matched the family parent hash for development-00 (EMSR578; `ed1c1b200a9eb3bb5cddc0b205ba6b2c9ad3cd52279bc84c63ab424a92024cb1`) and validation-00 (EMSR590; `c22c9b1116a5958d9efc9df5961dd6fc72c402a797a8a433793f5e674513d04a`). The reproducible generation command is `.venv/bin/python -m nightjar.geographic_family --raw-dir data/raw/cems --output-dir runs/geographic-family-real-v2`; it requires all four verified local products and a new or empty output directory. Development-only integration and benchmark CLI checks passed using the smoke and benchmark output paths documented in `README.md`. The full test suite passed (160 tests, 20 PyTorch-dependent skips); no reserved episodes, evaluations, or PPO training were run for this verification. This closes the geographic flat-landscape/data contract only, not the wider Stage 0 physical-validation gate or operational readiness.

Geographic PPO rejects duplicate scenario IDs in split files, even when the duplicate bytes and hashes match, and rejects missing or unlisted scenario membership. For real scenarios, source stage, ZIP digest, product code, filename, and image acquisition time must match the same registered catalog product. A smoke report identifies its development training pool (at most the first two loaded scenarios), rather than all scenarios available in the directory. Deterministic regression tests cover these checks with generated fixtures and catalog metadata; the smoke report test mocks training and does not establish PPO performance.

Supplied base mappings must use one of the four required activation IDs, matching the embedded `activation_id`, including in synthetic mode. Synthetic bases cannot carry a CEMS source ZIP checksum; real bases accept only an absent (inferred) or explicit `copernicus_cems` source kind. Rejection tests use deterministic generated fixtures and mocked catalog metadata; they establish identity/provenance checks, not source realism or physical validity.

The [Annaburroo diagnostic](VALIDATION_ANNABURROO.md) compares the unchanged FM1 head-rate equation with 120 measured grass-plot averages after one declared missing-data exclusion. Its 0.5264 m/s mean absolute error is a transfer diagnostic, not independent incident validation. The source version, license, table hashes, selection, command, and per-plot output are recorded there.

`examples/synthetic_observation_schedule.json` tests information timing and coverage on a synthetic arrival map. Its capture and delivery times are illustrative, not measurements of a real sensor. Tests confirm no change to the controller view from unobserved cells or undelivered reports. Real acquisition cadence, missed detections, and uncertainty distributions still need sourced definitions before interpreting a learned policy as relevant to real incidents.

`examples/synthetic_integrated.json` combines the FM1 grid, one aircraft, and a repeated full-grid survey. Every spatial input, aircraft speed, refill time, survey time, and `water_effect.radius_m: 35` is synthetic. The 35 m permanent barrier blocks future arrivals at cell centres but leaves already reached cells burning; it has no empirical water footprint, dose, or persistence basis. Its only legitimate use is an integration and evaluation smoke test. A sensitivity range for water effect, independent spread data, measured aircraft cycles, and observed drop response are still required before interpreting policy outcomes physically. The integrated replay records what the controller received, its orders, and the hidden outcome separately.

The exploratory scenario family keeps this base contract and explicitly varies ignition, wind, aircraft start, and the assumed water radius. Four base configurations are each paired at 35 and 55 m water radii, keeping all other inputs and the simulator seed equal within a pair. Its source is the committed synthetic example; none of its variants acquires real-world provenance through geographic naming or a different seed. Development and reserved splits test sensitivity within this simulator only. They are not independent incidents, and the reserved outcomes are already visible in the diagnostic report. A controller chosen after looking at them would need a new untouched evaluation set.

The separate decision challenge also derives from the committed synthetic example. Seeded `NONE` cells stand for hypothetical obstacles, not mapped nonburnable terrain. Its 30–150 s report delays are test settings, not measured sensor latencies; the 1,200 s horizon and wind remain synthetic. The four development cases were selected after exploratory baseline runs. The four separately seeded cases were kept without outcomes until the first learned checkpoint was selected; their outcomes were opened in a prior exploratory controller trial. They cannot be reused as untouched tests or establish physical transfer or independent incident performance.

Before policy training, test and document:

- Spread direction and rate response to wind, slope, fuel, and moisture, with units and plausible parameter ranges from identified fire-behavior literature or data.
- Sensitivity to spatial and temporal resolution, stochastic parameters, and boundary conditions.
- No-intervention fire evolution compared with independent observations at matching scale and timestamps. Report perimeter/area errors and uncertainty; do not calibrate and evaluate on the same incidents.
- Drop and refill mechanics: footprint, transit and service time, extinguishing or retardant effect, decay, and limits of evidence. If available evidence is insufficient, label the effect as a hypothesis and run sensitivity analyses.
- Resource movement and access: speed distributions, turn and approach constraints, refill location/cycle, and any intentionally omitted aircraft safety constraints.

An independently validated spread model is a prerequisite for strong claims about learned suppression. A policy should also be stress-tested across plausible parameter ensembles, since a single calibrated parameter set can reward simulator-specific behavior.

## 4. Observation validity and leakage audit

For every channel, specify when and by whom it becomes known. Test that changing latent fire or fuel **outside observed coverage** does not immediately change the controller input. Test that future weather, final burned area, and intervention outcomes are unavailable before their timestamps. The candidate-action generator, heuristic baselines, renderer exports, and normalization statistics are part of the leakage audit.

Observation latency is a parameterized distribution or schedule, not an annotation shown only in the UI. Missing data and sensor failures must be represented in the observation and included in evaluation.
